# Ask as one conversation (2026-11-16)

Why: older people do not manage conversations, do not think up a question before they start, and read a big
"This looks fair" band as noise. They show a thing, say a few words, and keep asking. WeKnow should answer like a
person: plain sentences, then offer the next questions as big buttons.

## The page
- Before the first message: the live camera fills the top, the input bar sits at the bottom (camera · words · mic).
- After the first message: one conversation, newest at the bottom, scrolling up to the start. The input bar stays at
  the bottom. The camera button opens the camera (or Photos / samples) and attaches the picture to the bar.
- Nothing is sent until Send. A picture and words go together, the way chat apps work. Speaking fills the box.
- Menu › Start again clears the conversation. Every answer is also kept in My checks, on the device.

## A message from the person
- Their picture (personal details covered once read) and their words, on the right.

## An answer from WeKnow
- On the left. No verdict band. The first sentence is the point, in plain words and bold:
  "This price is normal: $271 is within the usual $148 to $394."
  "Line 1 is $146 more than usual." / "Don't pay this bill yet." / "This sounds like a clogged kitchen sink."
- A small coloured tag only when it matters: amber "Careful" (a high price, a new payee), red "Stop" (a changed
  account, words aimed at an AI). Never green ticks on ordinary answers.
- Then the other sentences, the where-it-ran tag ("On this phone" / "Used the internet"), and the picture with its
  marks for a quote or a bill.
- Then 2–4 big buttons: the next questions. Pressing one is asking it (it shows as the person's message).

## Next-question buttons (plain code picks them)
| After | Buttons |
|---|---|
| A price (asked in words) | Can I do it myself? (if a guide points at this price) · I have a quote for this |
| A quote | What should I say to the shop? · Which lines did you not check? (if any) · Can I do any of it myself? (if a guide fits a line) |
| A bill | What should I do now? · Remember this company (a new payee with no warnings) |
| A problem it recognised | Show me the steps · How much would a pro charge? (if the guide names a pro price) |
| A problem it is not sure about | Up to three likely problems from the pack · None of these |
| Steps | Next step · Say it again · Stop; at the end: It's fixed · Still broken (→ the pro price) |
| Not sure what the picture is | It's a quote or a bill · Something is broken · Something else |

## Before answering a picture (on the device)
Several small on-device steps, each with one job: (1) what is in the picture (scene, from the image itself where the
model can see images), (2) the printed words, masked, (3) pack candidates for the lines or the problem, (4) which of
them fit. If (3)/(4) are not clearly one item, WeKnow asks with buttons instead of guessing. Nothing leaves the device
unless the person said yes to the internet, as before.

## Follow-ups keep the topic
A typed or spoken follow-up ("How much to fix it?") uses the last topic (the last item, guide, quote or bill) when
the words alone do not name one. A new picture starts a new topic in the same conversation.
